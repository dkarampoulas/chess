♞ Chess Data Science & Probability Theory CV / Portfolio Project

Project Title: Empirical Stochastic Modeling & Spatial Game-Theoretic Carnage in Competitive Rapid Chess

A rigorous data science and probability theory research project analyzing 2,500 real 10-minute rapid matches (44,166 total piece captures) from Lichess historical data.

Part 1: Duration Distribution Modeling

  • Dataset: Strictly 10+0 rapid games (600s initial clock, 0 increment), max bound = 20.0 minutes.
  • Base Model: Normal Distribution \(\mathcal{N}(\mu = 13.00 ext{ min}, \sigma = 5.34 ext{ min})\).
  • Model Selection: Rigorously evaluated against Gamma and Log-Normal via Log-Likelihood, AIC, BIC, and Kolmogorov-Smirnov test statistics.
  • Conditional Win Dynamics: Proved mathematically and empirically that White holds an early tactical initiative spike (\(>55\%\) win rate at \(t < 6\) min), while Draw rates surge 3x as games extend into deep King-and-Pawn endgames (\(t > 15\) min).

Part 2: Spatial Piece Deaths & Brawl Decisiveness

  • Spatial Probability: Calculated empirical probability \(p_{ ext{emp}}(i) = rac{D_i}{D_{ ext{total}}}\) across all 64 squares.
  • #1 Deadliest Square: D4 with 3,332 deaths (\(p_{ ext{emp}} = 7.54\%\)), followed by D5 (\(7.32\%\)), E5 (\(5.30\%\)), and E4 (\(4.67\%\)). The four central squares capture 24.8% of all fallen pieces on the entire board (4.8x uniform expectation).
  • Victim Demographics on D4: 67.4% Pawns, 19.2% Knights, 5.9% Bishops, 4.1% Queens, 3.4% Rooks.
  • Openings Engine: Open Sicilian Defense (e4 c5 Nf3 d6 d4 cxd4) and French Defense Advance breaks are the predominant catalysts of death on d4.
  • Brawl Correlation: When Black emerges victorious from the multi-capture exchange brawl on d4, Black wins the entire match 54.7% of the time.
